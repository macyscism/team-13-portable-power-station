01 — THE CHALLENGE

Power when the grid isn't available.

Power outages caused by severe weather and other disasters can leave people without access to essential electricity. Our team is developing a portable power station that combines solar generation, battery storage, power electronics, and user monitoring into one practical system.

PROJECT AT A GLANCE

Our preliminary design targets.

The team is developing a portable power station designed to balance energy capacity, output power, renewable charging, and practical operating time.

PROJECT PROGRESS

From project scope to subsystem design.

The project has progressed from initial planning and requirements toward a more detailed electrical system architecture. These highlights summarize submitted status reports and team notes from Weeks 1–5, documenting the project's progression from initial planning toward detailed subsystem design.

Fall 2026 Latest report: October 2, 2026
WEEK 1 Planning

Project Foundation

  • Selected the Portable Solar Emergency Power Station as the capstone project.
  • Established preliminary battery, AC output, solar input, and monitoring targets.
  • Created the initial work breakdown structure and two-semester project schedule.
  • Identified potential subject matter experts for electrical, embedded, and thermal design.
WEEK 2 Scope Refinement

Defining the Engineering Work

  • Refined the proposal following faculty feedback on the project's technical depth.
  • Established the team-designed MPPT converter, sensing circuits, embedded control, and firmware as important custom-design elements.
  • Identified thermal control and a team-developed local interface as additional design goals.
  • Left the final custom-design scope of the inverter and battery-management circuitry open for further guidance.
WEEK 3 Design Direction

MPPT Design Approved

  • Met with Dr. Geiger to discuss the revised technical scope.
  • Received approval to focus the primary custom power-electronics effort on the MPPT converter.
  • Divided project responsibilities so team members could begin research in parallel.
  • Identified the need to coordinate subsystem interfaces and obtain further SME guidance.
WEEK 4 Embedded Systems

Microcontroller Research & System Interfaces

  • Consulted Dr. Victor Nelson about embedded-system architecture and considerations for selecting a microcontroller.
  • Identified the need to define required measurements, system inputs and outputs, and the information that must be exchanged between subsystems.
  • Discussed STM32 and Arduino-compatible hardware, including controller familiarity, connectors, and expansion hardware.
  • Explored potential communication interfaces, including I²C, SPI, and CAN, along with controller memory, peripheral requirements, and floating-point capabilities.
  • Identified follow-up priorities: refine the system block diagram, continue researching MPPT implementation and related components, and develop a project cost and budget spreadsheet.
WEEK 5 System Architecture

Moving Toward Detailed Design

  • Developed a system-level block diagram showing major components and the flow of power and information.
  • Consulted Dr. Muljadi about MPPT operation and the hill-climbing control method.
  • Researched Texas Instruments reference designs and acquired a commercial solar charge controller for reference.
  • Identified PSCAD modeling and preliminary component selection as important next steps.

Current Technical Challenges

Translating the MPPT algorithm into a practical DC-DC converter requires coordinated decisions about the power stage, filtering, voltage and current sensing, control, and component selection.

The team must also ensure that the converter, battery, embedded controller, sensors, and display can operate together reliably. Component availability and the complexity of custom circuitry remain project risks.

Next Steps Documented in Week 5

  • Review the PSCAD modeling resources and renewable-energy materials from Dr. Muljadi.
  • Begin modeling the proposed MPPT system.
  • Continue researching converter design, filtering, sensing, and the hill-climbing algorithm.
  • Continue component research and develop a preliminary bill of materials.
