01 — THE CHALLENGE

Power when the grid isn't available.

Power outages caused by severe weather and other disasters can leave people without access to essential electricity. Our team is developing a portable power station that combines solar generation, battery storage, power electronics, and user monitoring into one practical system.

PROJECT AT A GLANCE

Our preliminary design targets.

The team is developing a portable power station designed to balance energy capacity, output power, renewable charging, and practical operating time.

≈500 Wh

Battery Energy

Target usable energy capacity using a lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO₄) battery.

≈300 W

AC Output

Target continuous AC power for compatible equipment and emergency loads.

150–200 W

Solar Input

Target solar input capability to recharge the battery using renewable energy.

≈4 Hours

Estimated Runtime

Preliminary runtime target at a 100 W average load, subject to system efficiency and operating conditions.

PROJECT PROGRESS • WEEK 5

From planning to detailed design.

Our team is moving from general project research toward detailed subsystem design. This update summarizes the work documented in our October 2, 2026 weekly status report.

Status Report: October 2, 2026 Focus: System Architecture & MPPT
01

Accomplishments

  • Developed a system-level block diagram showing the major components and how power and information flow through the system.
  • Met with Dr. Muljadi to discuss MPPT design and the hill-climbing control method.
  • Researched Texas Instruments reference designs to better understand converter circuitry and component selection.
  • Acquired a commercial solar charge controller to examine as a reference for our team-designed system.
02

Challenges & Risks

  • Translating MPPT theory into a practical DC-DC converter circuit.
  • Determining component values and selecting appropriate hardware.
  • Coordinating the power stage, filtering, voltage/current sensing, and control algorithm.
  • Reducing the risk of integration problems between the MPPT converter, battery, controller, sensors, and display.
03

Next Steps

  • Review the PSCAD modeling resources and renewable-energy materials provided by Dr. Muljadi.
  • Begin exploring a model of the proposed MPPT system.
  • Continue researching the hill-climbing algorithm, converter design, filtering, and voltage/current sensing.
  • Continue component research and develop a preliminary bill of materials.
