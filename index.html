THE CHALLENGE

Power when the grid isn't available.

When a storm knocks out the power, people may lose electricity for lights, phones, and other important devices. Our team is designing a portable power station that collects energy from sunlight, stores it in a battery, and makes that energy available when regular electricity is unavailable.

PROJECT AT A GLANCE

Our preliminary design targets.

The team is developing a portable power station designed to balance energy capacity, output power, renewable charging, and practical operating time.

≈300 W

SYSTEM DESIGN

One system. Multiple ways to stay powered.

01
☀

Solar Charging

Solar panels collect energy from sunlight and turn it into electricity that can be used to recharge the battery.

02
▣

Battery Storage

The battery stores the electricity collected from the solar panels so the power station can provide electricity at night or when sunlight isn't available.

03
ϟ

AC Inverter

The inverter changes the battery's electricity into the type of electricity used by regular household outlets, allowing the power station to run compatible devices.

04
USB

USB Outputs

USB ports let you charge phones, tablets, and other small electronic devices directly from the power station.

05
MPPT

MPPT Charger

MPPT stands for Maximum Power Point Tracking. It helps the solar charger adjust to changing sunlight so it can collect as much available energy as possible to recharge the battery.

06
BMS

Battery Management

The battery management system monitors the battery's condition and helps protect it from problems such as overheating, excessive current, or unsafe charging and discharging.

DESIGN PRIORITIES

Built for real-world use.

WEIGHT

Design the power station so one person can carry it easily during an emergency or outdoor trip.

RUNTIME

Choose a battery that can power devices for as long as possible without making the power station too heavy or expensive.

COST

Choose affordable components and keep the total prototype cost within our preliminary budget target of about $600.

THERMAL DESIGN

Manage heat generated by the power electronics and charging system.

Keep the battery and electrical components from getting too hot while the power station is charging or powering devices.

MECHANICAL ENCLOSURE

Design a sturdy case that holds and protects all the parts inside the power station. Make sure it is easy to carry and allows heat to escape so the electrical components can operate safely.

MOBILE MONITORING

Explore ways for users to check the battery level and power usage on their phones. This is an extra feature we will consider after the main power station is working reliably.

PROJECT PROGRESS

From project scope to subsystem design.

Building our power station takes more than picking parts and putting them together. Our team has been researching how the system should work, deciding which parts we need to design ourselves, and planning how everything will fit together. Here is a look at our progress so far.

Fall 2026 Latest report: October 2, 2026
WEEK 1 Planning

Project Foundation

  • Chose the Portable Solar Emergency Power Station as our senior design project.
  • Set initial goals for how much energy the battery should store, how much power the station should provide, and how it could recharge using sunlight.
  • Broke the project into smaller jobs and created a schedule for the two semesters.
  • Identified experts who could help us with electrical design, computer controls, and keeping the system cool.
WEEK 2 Scope Refinement

Defining the Engineering Work

  • Our instructors encouraged us to make sure the project involved designing our own electronics, rather than just connecting ready-made products.
  • We chose to focus on building our own solar charger that adjusts to sunlight to collect energy efficiently.
  • We also planned to develop the circuits and software that measure electrical conditions and help control the power station.
  • We still needed expert guidance to decide how much of the inverter and battery-protection systems we would design ourselves.
WEEK 3 Design Direction

MPPT Design Approved

  • Met with Dr. Geiger and received approval to focus our main electronics design effort on the solar-charging system.
  • Decided to develop our own solar charger that adjusts to changing sunlight to collect energy efficiently. This approach is called Maximum Power Point Tracking (MPPT).
  • Divided responsibilities so team members could research different parts of the project at the same time.
  • Identified the need to figure out how the different parts of the power station would connect and work together.
WEEK 4 Embedded Systems

Microcontroller Research & System Interfaces

  • Met with Dr. Victor Nelson to discuss the small computer that will help monitor and control our power station.
  • Identified the information the system needs to measure, such as voltage, current, and temperature, and the information it needs to display.
  • Compared possible controller boards, including STM32 and Arduino-compatible options.
  • Discussed how the different electronic parts could communicate with each other using methods such as I²C, SPI, and CAN.
  • Identified next steps, including improving our system diagram, researching the solar charger and its components, and developing a project budget.
WEEK 5 System Architecture

Moving Toward Detailed Design

  • Created a diagram showing how the solar panel, battery, power outputs, and control system are planned to connect.
  • Met with Dr. Muljadi to discuss how the solar charger can adjust to changing sunlight and collect energy efficiently.
  • Studied Texas Instruments design examples and obtained a commercial solar charge controller to learn from as a reference for our own design.
  • Identified computer simulations and choosing the right components as important next steps before building the solar charger.

Current Technical Challenges

One challenge is figuring out how to build a solar charger that adjusts to sunlight and safely sends electricity to the battery. Our team needs to choose the right electronic parts and determine how to measure the electricity moving through the system.

Another challenge is making sure the solar charger, battery, small control computer, sensors, and display all work together correctly. We also need to make sure the parts are available and the design stays within our time and budget.

Next Steps Documented in Week 5

  • Review the simulation tools and solar-energy materials recommended by Dr. Muljadi.
  • Begin creating a computer model of our solar charger so we can explore how it should work before building it.
  • Research how the charger adjusts to sunlight and how its electronic parts work together.
  • Continue choosing components and create a preliminary parts list with estimated costs.

PROJECT ORGANIZATION

How the work is organized.

Building a power station involves many different jobs. We have divided the project into seven areas so our team can work on different parts, keep track of what needs to be done, and bring everything together into one working system.

CAPSTONE PROJECT

Portable Solar Emergency Power Station

01

Power Electronics

Design and test the electronics that collect power from the solar panels and adjust it for charging the battery. Use computer simulations to help understand how the design should work before building the actual circuit.

02

Battery & BMS

Choose and test the battery that stores energy for the power station. Make sure the system can monitor the battery's condition and help protect it from unsafe charging, discharging, or excessive heat.

03

Embedded Control

Develop the small computer system that monitors the power station. It will collect information from sensors, track how the system is performing, and help control how the different parts work together.

04

Mechanical & Thermal Design

Investigate enclosure design, component placement, portability, electrical isolation, cooling, airflow, and thermal performance.

05

System Integration & Assembly

Combine the electrical, battery, embedded, output, and mechanical subsystems into one integrated prototype.

06

Testing & Verification

Verify subsystem operation, calibrate measurements, and evaluate efficiency, runtime, thermal behavior, and protection.

07

Project Documentation & Management

Maintain schedules, procurement records, design documentation, test results, risk tracking, reports, and team coordination.

DESIGN VALIDATION

How we'll verify our design.

Testing will begin at the individual circuit and subsystem levels before progressing to the fully integrated power station. Each stage will evaluate performance, reliability, and safe operation against the team's established design requirements.

✓

Verification starts with measurable results.

We will compare experimental measurements with design targets, record test results, and use the findings to guide design improvements before final system validation.

01 / POWER ELECTRONICS

MPPT Converter Performance

Evaluate the team-designed solar-charging converter and its control algorithm under different simulated or actual solar input conditions.

What we'll evaluate

  • MPPT tracking and response to changing input conditions
  • Converter output regulation and charging behavior
  • Power-conversion efficiency
  • Agreement between calculations, simulations, and measurements
02 / SENSING & CONTROL

Embedded Monitoring

Check whether the embedded controller can accurately collect, process, and display information from the power station's sensors.

What we'll evaluate

  • Voltage, current, and temperature measurement accuracy
  • Sensor readings against laboratory instruments
  • Power calculations and operating-status information
  • Runtime estimates, warnings, and thermal-control responses
03 / BATTERY & OUTPUTS

Storage & Power Delivery

Evaluate battery behavior and confirm that the power station can deliver useful electrical power through its planned outputs.

What we'll evaluate

  • Battery charging, discharging, and usable energy
  • Protection against abnormal electrical and temperature conditions
  • AC output voltage and performance under increasing loads
  • USB output voltage and current capability
04 / SYSTEM VALIDATION

Runtime & Thermal Performance

Test the integrated prototype under representative operating conditions to evaluate performance over time.

What we'll evaluate

  • Runtime at representative electrical loads
  • Progress toward the preliminary four-hour target at a 100 W load
  • Component temperatures and enclosure cooling
  • Integrated solar charging, battery storage, outputs, and monitoring
FINAL VALIDATION

From individual circuits to a complete system.

After the individual subsystems have been evaluated, the team will test the integrated power station to verify solar charging, battery operation, AC and USB outputs, monitoring, protection, runtime, and thermal performance as a complete system.

Test procedures, expected values, measured results, and any corrective design changes will be documented as verification progresses.
